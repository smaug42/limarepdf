# Glossary

The PDF words this guide uses, in plain terms.

**Annotation.** Something added on top of a page rather than into it: a comment, sticky note, highlight, text box,
shape, drawing or stamp. Other apps can show, move or remove annotations. Flattening one makes it part of the page.
See [Comments and markup](comments-and-markup.md).

**Arrow.** The tool for selecting, moving and resizing anything on the page, lines of page text and full-page
scans included, one or several at a time; in Edit > Select (or ☰ > Tools > Select, move and resize objects). See
[Editing text and pictures](content-editing.md#select-several-objects).

**Bates number.** A number stamped on every page of a set of documents, such as `ABC000123`, so that each page can
be referred to exactly. Common in legal work.

**Bookmark.** An entry in a document's table of contents (its outline), shown in the Bookmarks view; clicking one
goes to its page.

**Certificate.** The public part of a digital ID: who it belongs to, who issued it and until when it is valid. It
goes into every signature made with the ID, so others can check the signature.

**Certificate authority.** An organisation that issues certificates and vouches for who holds them. Trusting an
authority trusts every certificate it issued.

**Certify.** To sign a document as its author, saying which changes others may still make (such as filling in the
form). A document can be certified only before anyone else signs it.

**Command band.** The names Edit, Insert, Review, Pages, Forms and sign, Protect and View under the document tabs,
and below them the buttons of the name chosen, in groups. This guide writes a button as name > button, such as
Pages > Split. See [The command band](gui.md#the-command-band).

**Command palette.** The list of every command, opened with Ctrl+K: type to find one, Enter to run it.

**Compact commands.** The command band on one row of icons, which name themselves on hover. The small arrow at the
right of the commands row, or View > Show > Compact commands, turns it on and off; windows under 600 points high
show the commands compact by themselves.

**Crop.** Trimming the margins of a page as it is shown. The content outside is hidden, not deleted.

**Digital ID.** What you sign digital signatures with: a certificate and the private key that goes with it, kept
in a password-protected file. You can make your own in LimarePDF or import one from an organisation.

**Digital signature.** A signature made with a digital ID, which proves the part of the document it signed hasn't
changed since that ID signed it, and shows any change saved afterwards. Who signed is only as certain as the
signer's certificate: anyone can make an ID with any name, so the name is confirmed only when you trust the
certificate (see Trust list). Different from a picture of your signature (see Fill and sign).

**Embedded font.** A font stored inside the PDF, so the text looks the same on any computer. Text LimarePDF writes in
Helvetica, Times or Courier with only Western European characters uses the PDF standard font of that name, which
every reader has and which isn't embedded. Other text is written in an embedded font, cut down to the characters
used: Liberation (for characters the standard fonts lack), or the installed font it is typed in (which Chinese,
Japanese, Korean and emoji always are).

**Fill and sign.** Putting your signature, initials, the date or a tick on a document as pictures and text, as you
would with a pen on paper. Not a digital signature: it doesn't prove who signed.

**Flatten.** To draw annotations or form fields into the page as they look now. They stop being annotations or
fields: a field can no longer be filled in, and a comment can no longer be changed or removed as a comment, in any
reader. What was drawn is ordinary page content, which content editing can still move, resize or delete as a whole
(see [Move, resize or delete](content-editing.md#move-resize-or-delete)).

**Form field.** A box in a form to fill in: a text field, checkbox, radio button or list.

**Header and footer.** Text repeated at the top or bottom of every page, such as page numbers or a date.

**Hidden information.** What a document carries that isn't on any page: its properties (metadata), scripts,
attached files, hidden layers, thumbnails and the like. Removing hidden information takes it out before you share
the document. See [Redaction](redaction.md#removing-hidden-information).

**Icon strip.** The icons at the window's left edge: Pages, Search results, Bookmarks, Comments and Digital
signatures. Each opens that view in the side panel beside it; clicking the view shown again folds the panel away.

**Inspector.** The panel on the right of the window, which shows the options for what is selected, or for the
whole document when nothing is. Tools such as Watermark and Crop open in it on their own, with a × to close them.

**Metadata.** Information about the document stored in it, such as its title, author and the program that made it.

**OCR (text recognition).** Reading the text in a scan or photo, so that it can be searched and copied. LimarePDF
adds the recognised text as an invisible layer over the picture.

**Open password.** A password needed to open a document. Also called a user password.

**Page labels.** The page numbers a viewer shows, such as i, ii, 1, 2 or A-1. They don't change what is printed on
the pages.

**Permissions password.** A password that limits what others may do with a document (print, copy, change it)
without stopping them from opening it. Also called an owner password.

**Planned.** The mark on a greyed button for a feature not built yet, shown where it will be. It does nothing.
In the inspector, Future text tools carries the mark too. Turning off Show planned features, in the status bar or
View > Show, hides them.

**Read tool.** The tool every document opens with (the text cursor): it selects text, follows links, fills in
forms, and selects and moves pictures, shapes and comments on the page (Shift+click for several pictures and
shapes).

**Recovery copy.** A private copy LimarePDF keeps of a document with unsaved changes, so it can offer the document
back if the app stops unexpectedly. It never changes your own file.

**Redaction.** Removing content for good: the text, pictures and drawings under a marked area are taken out of the
file, and the area is painted black. Unlike covering something with a black box, nothing can bring it back.

**Signed version.** The document exactly as it was when a signature was made. Changes saved after signing are
added on top of it, so the signed version can always be taken out again.

**Stamp.** A mark such as Approved, Draft or Confidential, or your own wording, put on a page as an annotation.

**Thumbnail.** A small picture of a page, in the Pages view on the left.

**Trust list.** The signers and certificate authorities you have told LimarePDF to trust. Only signatures from
them show as trusted.

**Watermark.** Text or a picture shown on every page, such as DRAFT across the middle.

**XFA form.** A kind of form made for Adobe Reader, which only Adobe software can show or fill in.
